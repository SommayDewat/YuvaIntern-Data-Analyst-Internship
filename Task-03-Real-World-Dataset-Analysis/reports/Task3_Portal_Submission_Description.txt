This project analyses the Seoul Bike Sharing Demand dataset to understand hourly rental behaviour and the relationship between demand, time and weather conditions. The original dataset contains 8,760 hourly observations and 14 columns, covering 1 December 2017 to 30 November 2018. The analysis begins with dataset inspection, column and data-type review, descriptive statistics, and data-quality checks. The preprocessing stage standardizes column names, parses dates using the correct day/month/year format, converts numeric variables, trims categorical values, and derives month, weekday and weekday/weekend features. The original source file is preserved.

Data quality assessment checks missing values, exact duplicate rows, duplicate date-hour keys, invalid dates, negative rental counts, out-of-range hours and humidity, negative weather measurements, and unexpected categorical values. The dataset has no missing cells or duplicate records, and the documented domain checks identify no invalid values. The IQR method flags 158 rental observations as statistically unusual; these are retained rather than automatically removed because an outlier is not necessarily a data error.

Exploratory analysis includes rental distributions and descriptive statistics, hourly demand, seasonal demand, weekday versus weekend demand, functioning-day comparisons, weather relationships and Pearson correlations. Visualizations include histograms, line charts, bar charts, boxplots, scatterplots and a correlation heatmap. The results show 6,172,314 total rentals, average hourly demand of 704.60, a peak average hour of 18:00, and summer average demand 4.58 times winter demand. Weekday average demand is 7.8% higher than weekend demand. Temperature is positively associated with rentals (r=0.539), while humidity is negatively associated (r=-0.200).

The report interprets each major chart, summarizes business implications, and provides recommendations for peak-hour readiness, seasonal planning, weather-aware monitoring and outlier investigation. It also documents limitations: one year of data, no station-level analysis, no trip-purpose or customer information, and no causal inference. The notebook is reproducible and the results folder contains the cleaned dataset, quality report, summary tables and figures. These are descriptive findings, not a validated forecasting model.
